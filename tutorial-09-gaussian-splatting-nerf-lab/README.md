## Run and verify

Buildless static HTML/JS, with no app dependencies. Serve `dist/` using `python3 -m http.server 4173 --directory dist`. Node.js is needed for `npm run check` and `npm test`. Sections 1–6 work without remote assets. Section 7 loads a credited external WebGL viewer and scene on demand. Question attempts use sessionStorage, with graceful fallback when storage is unavailable.

Numerical tests cover least squares, covariance/projection/compositing, analytic field gradients, isosurface extraction, single-view ambiguity, primitive ray intersections, image-based gradients, reserved-image exclusion, fitting, view coverage, initialization budgets, isotropy, and density refinement. Browser interactions are covered by `tests/browser_tutorial_qa.py` (requires Playwright and the local server). It checks all seven sections, geometry handoff, sliders, fitting, independent inspection views, reconstruction budgets and evaluation, all fourteen answers, keyboard navigation, mobile overflow, legacy routes, and the lazy Garden iframe URL. It intercepts the external iframe: full Garden streaming/GPU rendering is not part of that test.

## Seven-section progression

1. **Approximate in 1D** (`#mixture`): manually place Gaussian means, widths, and signed amplitudes. Fit amplitudes by linear least squares with a pivoted QR solve. Compare target, sum, components, residual, RMSE/SSE, and optimal prefix-basis error versus N. Initial bases are nested; amplitudes are unconstrained function coefficients, not normalized probabilities.
2. **Extend to 2D** (`#dimensions`): vector means, anisotropic covariance, rotation, target/approximation/error maps, and a draggable height field. LSE solves amplitudes on a 31×31 sample grid. Display height represents scalar output, not a third input coordinate.
3. **Fit a 3D function** (`#parameters`): directly approximate a scalar field over (x,y,z), initially with one Gaussian. Choose a rounded box, Gaussian bump, or ring target; edit means, three scales, world-z rotation, and amplitude; increase N up to 8. Adam updates geometry and amplitudes from 15³ scalar samples. LSE updates amplitudes only. A shared-isovalue target/fit comparison and linked XY/XZ/YZ slices expose errors throughout the volume. Rotating the inspection view does not affect the observations or objective. No camera rendering or colour is introduced here.
4. **Add colour + opacity** (`#colour`): add RGB and opacity, project the covariance, and alpha-composite the splats. The previous section’s Next button carries geometry forward and assigns fresh appearance parameters; scalar amplitudes are explicitly not converted to opacity. The linked selected ellipsoid/camera rig and rendered image show the same geometry. A sampled-pixel table reports depth, alpha, transmittance, and weight. Isolate one Gaussian, then scale its camera-relative mean and all scales by 1.25: its locally projected footprint stays unchanged from that camera, while its 3D position changes. Orbit to expose the ambiguity.
5. **Reconstruct from images** (`#reconstruction`): twelve views of a plane, box, sphere, and thin cylinder. Targets use an independent opaque surface ray tracer with analytic intersections, Lambertian shading, shadows, and four subpixel rays. They are not Gaussian renderings. A 64-point noisy surface cloud stands in for upstream SfM. Select an initial budget of 12/24/48/64 (default 24), select training views, and fit a shared Gaussian scene. Compare target/prediction/error, training MSE, reserved-view MSE, seed/initial/current/reference displays, and a free camera. One/nearby/spread-view presets reset identically. After 10 updates, density refinement can prune/split/clone, followed by more fitting. Error at sharp edges, the thin post, shadows, and occlusions motivates capacity, observation, and optimization limits.
6. **Compare with NeRF** (`#nerf`): a trainable neural radiance field over a clearly labeled 2D spatial cross-section. A real 970-parameter MLP maps two-band positional features through two 24-unit tanh layers to softplus density; its sigmoid RGB head also receives ray direction. Camera rows contain 33 pixels, with three training poses and a permanently reserved 0° pose. Adam fits network weights through analytic volume-rendering gradients using RGB observations only. Inspect target/learned field maps, select a ray, compare camera rows, vary midpoint quadrature from 8 to 64 samples, and follow density, segment alpha, transmittance, and weights in linked plots and a table. Training-set changes restart the same initialization. Sampling changes preserve weights. The section compares neural-field queries with Gaussian rasterization and distinguishes sampling from adding primitives. This is a small 2D teaching model, not the original full 3D NeRF training system.
7. **Explore a real garden** (`#highres`): load the real captured Garden scene from Mip-NeRF 360 using [Kevin Kwok’s original viewer](https://github.com/antimatter15/splat). Approximately 187 MB / 5.8 million Gaussians, streamed from the provider. The embedded viewer, direct original-viewer link, fullscreen, reload, credits, and inspection prompts remain together. No model or viewer code is redistributed. This is pretrained rendering, not browser training; network and WebGL are required. The compact scene format uses RGB rather than full view-dependent spherical harmonics.

Each section has two master-level diagnostic questions (14 total): a concrete scenario, plausible misconceptions, optional hint, choice-specific feedback, a further reasoning prompt, worked derivation, and a controlled experiment using the existing demonstration. The questions address identifiability, signed expansion weights, covariance eigenstructure, normalization, gradient flow, approximation capacity, visibility, multi-view geometry, experimental controls, density refinement, directional appearance, and geometric validation. There is one seven-item navigation, with no extra 3.5/3.6 pages. Arrow/Home/End keys navigate tabs. Sliders, questions, and inspection canvases support keyboard use. Existing links to `#projection` and `#compositing` lead to section 4; `#density` leads to section 5; `#optimization` leads to section 3. The default opening is `#mixture`.

## Mathematical conventions and teaching limits

World y is up. Camera x is right, y down, and positive z forward. Section 4 uses a 720×380 reference image, focal length 600, and principal point (360,190). Reconstruction uses 80×60 images and focal length 84. Covariance is `R diag(s²) Rᵀ`; scales are standard deviations. Rotation is restricted to the world z-axis. Spatial kernels are unnormalized, with peak one; function amplitudes may be signed. Opacity is a separate bounded parameter in the rendering model.

Projected covariance uses the local pinhole Jacobian plus `0.3 I` pixel filtering. Rendering sorts Gaussian centres by camera depth, with Gaussian footprints truncated at approximately four standard deviations. This approximates visibility. RGB arithmetic is linear; displayed values map directly to browser RGB without a calibrated colour pipeline. The linked ellipsoid close-up follows the selected mean at a fixed world scale; the separate camera rig shows actual relative positions at its own scale. Shading on that ellipsoid is an inspection aid, not its opacity.

Section 3 fits sampled scalar values, using analytic mean/log-scale/angle/amplitude derivatives and bounded Adam updates. Isosurfaces use marching tetrahedra on a 16³ cell grid; slices evaluate the actual functions. An isosurface match alone does not establish a match throughout the field. The default non-Gaussian field exposes a single kernel’s approximation limits.

Section 5’s optimizer receives only images, calibrated cameras, and noisy surface seeds. Reference primitives are available separately for drawing the comparison diagram; they are not supplied as fitting targets or parameter labels. The fitter differentiates Gaussian image formation, uses central differences for the small projection Jacobian, and applies Adam to 3D means, log-scales, a z-axis angle, logit opacity, and RGB. Image supervision uses MSE and constant RGB. The scene is intentionally bounded and low resolution. Full SfM, the original 3DGS objective, GPU training, and spherical harmonics are outside scope.

Frame 10 is permanently excluded inside the optimizer as well as disabled in the UI. Evaluation never contributes a gradient. Camera tours change only the inspected frame; independent inspection changes only the diagram. Coverage, initial budget, or shape-mode changes restart from the same seed prefix. Round-only mode ties the three scales and fixes rotation. Density refinement uses current training-image gradients and a size heuristic, capped at 64 Gaussians. Child initialization need not lower loss. The initial 400-update limit can be extended by another 100 updates after refinement. The separated-view improvement in the deterministic test fixture is evidence for that fixture, not a guarantee for every scene or initialization.
